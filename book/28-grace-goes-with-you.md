# Chapter Twenty-Eight

## Grace Goes With You

Read the last line of the Bible and notice what it is, and what it is not.

Sixty-six books. A law thundered from a mountain, prophets shouting in the streets,
a nation carried off and brought home, four Gospels, the letters of a man who was
beaten half to death for writing them, visions of thrones and seals and plagues, a
city coming down out of heaven, a renewed creation where every tear is wiped away.
And then the book ends, and the final sentence — the one all of it has been moving
toward — is not a command. It is not a warning. It is not a summary of doctrine or a
list of things to do.

The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with all the saints. Amen.

A blessing, spoken over the reader as the page goes quiet.

Grace gets the last word over the whole book. And that is not an accident of
editing; it is the shape of the thing. Grace was the first word too — the God who
made a world nobody had earned and walked in it in the cool of the day — and
everything between the two is the story of that grace surviving every attempt to
lose it.

---

This book has been about a shape: disconnect, disorientation, reorientation,
reconnect. I want to end by saying what the shape is not.

**It is not a promise about your timeline.** Nothing here guarantees that your
disorientation ends this year, or that you will personally see the reorientation of
the Church you love. Augustine died in a besieged city. Jeremiah died in Egypt
against his will. Bonhoeffer was hanged three weeks before the war ended. Moses got
the ridge and not the land. There is a long line of people who did the faithful
thing inside a phase they never saw the end of, and it is not a line of failures.

**It is not an explanation.** Having a map does not tell you why. Job asked and did
not get an answer, and Habakkuk asked and got a horizon instead of a timetable, and
Paul asked three times and got a sentence about grace being sufficient. If you have
read this whole book hoping it would eventually explain what happened to you, I am
sorry. That was never on offer, from me or from Scripture.

**And it is not a technique.** You cannot make the phases go faster by understanding
them better. The map does not shorten the journey. It only tells you that the
featureless middle is a place on the route and not the end of the road, which on
some nights is the entire difference between staying and quitting.

---

So what is it for?

It is for the fourteenth month. That is the honest answer. It is for three in the
morning when you have lost all sense of whether anything is happening at all, and
you need somebody to say: this is a country, other people have crossed it, it has an
exit, you are not being punished and you are not going mad.

And it is for the recognition that your private shaking and the Church's enormous
one are the same shape, which means you are not a casualty of a moment in history so
much as a participant in it. Whatever you are living through in your own life is
being lived through, in its own way, by a body of believers spread across the world
who are asking the same question you are: where now is the authority, and what
remains when everything that can be shaken has gone.

---

A few things to take out the door, gathered from the whole book, in the plainest
form I can manage.

**Locate yourself, and stop taking advice for a phase you are not in.**

**In the break: count the cost, and check what you are keeping viable.** Most breaks
fail because the old thing was left running.

**In the wilderness: stay, and keep a floor.** Not a ceiling — a floor. The minimum
you will do regardless of how it feels. Eat and sleep before you diagnose your soul.
Tell one person the truth regularly. Wait sixty days before acting on anything that
promises to end the ambiguity.

**In reorientation: look backwards to see the turn, sort what is foundation from
what is not, and rebuild the section of wall in front of your own house.** Do not
leave the wilderness with a new certainty. Leave it with a truer humility and a
shorter first column.

**In reconnection: go to the table before you feel ready, and go small.** Two or
three people who know the actual story. And when the wound has become a scar, go
back and tell somebody what was done for you.

**And under all four: God shakes what He means to keep.** He is not the author of
the evil that was done to you, and He is Lord of the wreckage anyway, and both of
those are true at once and always will be.

## In You

If you are still in the middle of it, this last page is for you specifically.

You may have read this whole book from inside a shaking that has not stopped, and
noticed that the chapters got progressively less applicable — that Part One
described you and Part Four described somebody else. That is not a failure of your
faith or of your effort. It is where you are.

Here is what I would want said to me, in that position.

The fact that you read a book about upheaval is itself evidence of something. People
who have genuinely given up do not do that. The ache you have been calling doubt,
or failure, or a loss of faith, is very often the opposite — it is a soul still
reaching for something it can no longer feel, which is precisely what the tradition
has always identified as the mark of someone God is holding on to rather than
someone He has let go.

You do not have to feel any of this to be true. That is worth repeating, because
almost everything in Christian culture has trained you to check your feelings for
confirmation. Your sense of God's presence is a measurement of your sense. The
kingdom you are receiving is not being handed over on the strength of your capacity
to perceive it.

And you are not behind. There is no schedule you are failing to meet. The forty
years, the seventy years, the nine months in a cell, the four hundred years of
silence between the last prophet and the angel in the temple — the whole record is
of a God who works at a pace that looks like neglect from inside and looks like
patience from the ridge.

## In the Body

And a last word about the Church, since this book has insisted the whole way that
the two cannot be separated.

The Body is in the middle of the largest reordering of its life in five hundred
years, and nobody alive will see the end of it. That is a hard sentence and it is
also a liberating one, because it removes an impossible burden. You are not
responsible for the outcome of a rummage sale. Nobody in 1517 was responsible for
1650. What each generation is responsible for is its own section of wall.

The historical record offers one consistent consolation and one consistent warning.

The consolation: the Church has been emptied onto the lawn four times, has looked
terminal from inside every one of them, and has come out of each more vital and more
widespread than it went in. Renewal has never once arrived from the direction anyone
was watching, and it has almost always come through somebody small and unappointed.

The warning: none of that happened automatically, and a great many of the casualties
were caused by Christians. The Church is not owed its renewal. It has received it,
repeatedly, as grace — which is the only way anyone has ever received anything in
this story.

## What Cannot Be Shaken

Grace. That is the last word, and it is the last word for a reason.

When the whole journey is over — the break, the wilderness, the slow rebuild, the
table — and you close the book and step out into an ordinary Tuesday, this is what
walks out of the door with you. Not your performance in the shaking, which was
uneven. Not your record, which you know. Not even the strength of your faith, which
has been the least reliable instrument you own.

The grace of the Lord Jesus. Given freely, first word and last, over a person who
did not earn the beginning of it and cannot lose the end of it.

The upheaval was real. It cost what it cost, and this book has tried never to
pretend otherwise. But it was never the end of the story.

The kingdom that cannot be shaken is.

And its grace goes with you.
