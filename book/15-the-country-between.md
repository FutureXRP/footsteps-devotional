# Chapter Fifteen

## The Country Between

He is under a bush in the desert asking God to kill him, and it is the day after
the greatest victory of his career.

That is the detail that makes Elijah so useful. Carmel had gone perfectly. Fire
from heaven, four hundred and fifty prophets of Baal exposed in front of the whole
nation, the rain returning after three years of drought, and Elijah running ahead of
the king's chariot in the storm with the strength of God on him. It was total
vindication.

Then Jezebel sends one message — by this time tomorrow you will be as dead as they
are — and he runs. A day's journey into the wilderness, and he sits under a broom
tree and asks to die. It is enough, he says. Take my life; I am no better than my
fathers.

And God's response is the most surprising thing in the passage, because it is not a
response at all. No rebuke. No reminder of yesterday. An angel touches him and
tells him to eat, and there is bread and a jar of water, and he eats and sleeps.
Then the angel comes back and does it again — get up and eat, because the journey
is too much for you.

Sleep and food, twice, before a single word of theology.

If you take one thing from this chapter, take that. The first ministry to a person
collapsing in the wilderness is not insight. It is a meal and a nap.

---

William Bridges, writing about how organisations and people actually get through
change, called this stretch the neutral zone — the country between an ending and a
new beginning. His argument, which has been proved on a thousand failed corporate
reorganisations, was that this middle is where the real work happens and that
everyone tries to skip it, and that skipping it is why so much change does not take.

Scripture says the same thing with sand in it. But the practical description is
useful, because the neutral zone has recognisable behaviours and if you can name
them you can stop being frightened of them.

**Nothing seems to be happening.** This is the defining feature. You are doing all
the things and there is no visible movement, and the sense of wasting your life
becomes acute. Israel spent thirty-eight of its forty years going essentially
nowhere.

**Old motivations stop working.** Things that used to drive you — ambition,
approval, the momentum of a plan — go inert. This is often mistaken for depression
and is sometimes actually depression, so read the previous chapter's caution again.

**Anxiety rises and competence drops.** People in the neutral zone make bad
decisions, forget things, and are unusually vulnerable to anyone offering certainty.

**And it is where the real formation occurs.** Everything the wilderness generation
learned — manna, water from rock, that the LORD was with them without a temple, the
whole law — they learned in the years when nothing was happening.

---

There are four exits from this country and three of them are wrong.

**Going back.** Covered already, and it remains the most popular.

**Forcing a premature new beginning.** This is the golden calf. Moses is up the
mountain, gone longer than expected, and the people cannot bear the ambiguity — so
they make something. Note that they were not trying to abandon God; Aaron announces
a feast to the LORD. They were trying to make the invisible God visible and
manageable, on a timeline they could stand. An idol is very often not a rejection of
God. It is an attempt to get God on a schedule.

Watch for it in yourself. A new relationship at month eight. A sudden certainty
adopted whole from a confident teacher. A dramatic decision that mostly functions
to end the ambiguity. Anything that arrives with enormous relief in the middle of a
wilderness deserves a hard look, because relief is what the calf was for.

**Setting up permanent residence.** This one is subtle and it is the exit that
disguises itself as depth. There are people who went into a wilderness fifteen years
ago and have made an identity out of it. Deconstruction became a personality;
woundedness became a position; the questions became a permanent home because the
questions could not fail them the way answers had. God's word to Israel at Sinai —
Sinai, the mountain where He met them — was that they had stayed long enough at
this mountain, and it was time to break camp. Even the good stops are stops.

**And the fourth exit is the one you cannot schedule**, which is that the cloud
moves. That is the only legitimate one, and it is not under your control, and the
whole discipline of this phase is remaining available for it.

---

Two provisions were given for the trackless waste, and they are worth naming
because they are what you actually live on.

The first is manna, and its most important feature is that it rotted. Gather what
you need for today; keep it overnight and it breeds worms and stinks. They were fed
for forty years by a God who would not let them stockpile Him. Every single morning
they had to go out again, and the arrangement seems designed to make an ongoing
dependence the only available way to live.

If you are unable to see how you will manage six months from now, you are not
failing at faith. You are on manna. Nobody has ever been given six months of it at
once.

The second is company, and it is under-mentioned. Two are better than one, says the
Preacher, in a book that is otherwise unremittingly bleak about everything under the
sun — because if they fall, one will lift up his fellow, but woe to him who is alone
when he falls, for he has no one to help him up. Elijah, who thought he was the only
one left, was told there were seven thousand he did not know about, and then given
Elisha. Moses, at the point of collapse, was given seventy elders. The wilderness is
where people isolate, and isolation is the single most reliable way to make this
phase worse.

## In You

Practical, then, because this phase is survived by practice rather than
understanding.

**Eat and sleep.** Genuinely. Before you interrogate your spiritual condition, ask
when you last had a full night's sleep and a proper meal. An enormous proportion of
what presents as a crisis of faith at 2 a.m. is a crisis of blood sugar and
exhaustion, and the angel dealt with Elijah's body twice before dealing with
anything else.

**Reduce the load.** You are operating at diminished capacity, and pretending
otherwise is how people in the neutral zone wreck things. Fewer commitments. Fewer
decisions. Postpone anything reversible.

**Keep a floor, not a ceiling.** Decide the minimum you will do — a psalm, five
minutes, one gathering a week — and do not let a bad month raise or lower it. The
floor is the whole practice here.

**Say it out loud to one person.** Not a broadcast. One person, told the actual
truth, regularly. This is the single highest-return action available in this phase
and it is the one almost everyone skips.

**Watch for the calf.** When something arrives promising to end the ambiguity, wait
sixty days before acting on it. If it is real it will survive sixty days.

**And write things down.** In the fog you will not be able to tell whether anything
is happening, because the movement in this phase is too slow to perceive day to day.
A journal is not a spiritual discipline here; it is instrumentation. In a year you
will read it and see the shape you could not see while you were in it.

## In the Body

The Church's neutral zone behaviours are the same, scaled up, and recognisable in
almost every Western denomination right now.

**Nothing seems to be happening,** and so leaders are under enormous pressure to
produce visible movement — which is precisely the pressure that manufactures
golden calves. The last thirty years have produced a great many of them:
initiatives, rebrands, strategic plans, movements announced with a conference and
dissolved within a decade. Some were faithful attempts. Many were the body's
inability to sit still.

**Old motivations have stopped working.** Guilt does not fill buildings any more.
Duty does not. Institutional loyalty does not. The levers that worked in 1965 are
connected to nothing, and a lot of institutional exhaustion is the fatigue of
pulling them anyway.

**And there is a real temptation to permanent residence** — a Church so at home in
its own critique that it can describe its condition with great sophistication and
has stopped expecting God to do anything. There is a version of the deconstructed,
post-evangelical, exquisitely self-aware church that has been camped at the mountain
for twenty years. Long enough at this mountain.

What the Body actually needs in the neutral zone is unglamorous and it is exactly
what Benedict provided after Rome: a floor. Not a strategy. A rule — practices that
continue regardless of results. Scripture read publicly. The table kept. The poor
fed. The prayers said. Baptisms when they come. The historical record is remarkably
consistent on this point: the Christian communities that came through long
disorientations were the ones with a floor, and the ones that collapsed were the ones
whose life depended on conditions that stopped.

## What Cannot Be Shaken

Deuteronomy notices something absurd about the forty years, and mentions it twice.
Their clothes did not wear out. Their feet did not swell.

It is such a small thing to record about four decades. Not the fire, not the law,
not the conquest — the shoes held. And that is precisely why it is there. In the
years when nothing was happening, when there was no progress to report and no story
worth telling, something was being quietly done for them every single day that they
noticed only in retrospect.

What cannot be shaken is the keeping. Not the visible provision — the manna stopped
the day they ate the food of the land, and the cloud eventually lifted for good. The
keeping is what remains: a God who sustained a people through the most featureless
stretch of their history, so unremarkably that it took a farewell speech forty years
later for anyone to notice.

You are being kept. You will probably only be able to see it later.

## For Reflection

1. When did you last sleep properly and eat properly? Answer before you diagnose
   your soul.
2. What is your floor — the minimum you will keep regardless of how it feels?
3. Is there a golden calf on offer right now, promising to end the ambiguity? What
   would waiting sixty days cost you?
