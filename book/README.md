# The Upheaval — the book

A single-volume book distilled from *The Upheaval*, the 365-day devotional series
(`data/series/the-upheaval.json`, ~279,000 words). Where the devotional is a year
of daily entries, this is a continuous read: one argument, carried from the first
crack to the far side.

**Target length:** ~50,000 words
**Spine:** Disconnect → Disorientation → Reorientation → Reconnect, framed by the
promise of Hebrews 12:26–27 and closed by what the shaking could not reach.

## Chapter shape

Every chapter runs the same four beats, deliberately varied in length and order so
the form never hardens into a template:

1. **A scene.** A person, a place, a time. Never an exposition of a verse.
2. **The turn.** What the scene means for anyone living through a shaking.
3. **In You** — the reader's own upheaval. Second person. Self-contained.
4. **In the Body** — the upheaval of the Church as a whole: Western and global
   Christianity in this moment, whole movements and traditions, the Church before
   a watching world. Never one local congregation.
5. **What Cannot Be Shaken** — the specific enduring thing this chapter surfaced,
   named freshly each time.
6. **For Reflection** — three questions.

The two middle sections are never blurred into each other. That separation is the
governing rule of the series and it carries into the book.

## Authoring constraints inherited from the series

See `docs/the-upheaval-principles.md`. The load-bearing ones:

- **Scripture:** World English Bible (public domain). The divine name is rendered
  *the LORD*, never Yahweh or Jehovah.
- **Copyright:** Phyllis Tickle, Bill Hybels, Scot McKnight, Walter Brueggemann,
  William Bridges, Karl Barth, Dietrich Bonhoeffer and any living author are
  **paraphrased and attributed, never quoted**. Pre-1929 figures and Scripture may
  be quoted freely.
- **Providence without authoring evil.** Where a chapter touches inflicted evil or
  catastrophic loss, it distinguishes God's redeeming sovereignty from authorship
  of the evil.
- **Prose carries no double-quote characters** — the house style of the series.
- **Plain language.** *The church*, not *ecclesial*. The door stays wide for newer
  believers.
