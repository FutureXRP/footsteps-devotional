# Chapter Eight

## Into the Sea

They are trapped, and they know it.

Behind them, a dust cloud on the horizon that is resolving into chariots — six
hundred of them, according to the account, with Pharaoh himself in the column.
Ahead of them, water. Not a river to be forded; a sea, wide and deep and entirely
impassable to a nation travelling on foot with children and livestock. On either
side, terrain that offers nothing.

This is the geometry of Disconnect in its purest form. There is no road back,
because the road back is filled with the army you left, and going back means
returning to slavery under a man who has just been publicly humiliated. And there
is no road forward, because forward is water.

Israel does the human thing. They turn on Moses with the sarcasm of the terrified —
was it because there were no graves in Egypt that you brought us out here to die?
They have been free for approximately a week and they are already arguing that
slavery was better, which is a pattern this chapter will come back to.

And Moses says the sentence that has steadied cornered people ever since. Do not be
afraid. Stand still, and see the salvation of the LORD.

Then the wind comes. All night, a strong east wind, and the water opens, and by
morning there is a corridor of dry ground with the sea standing up on either hand.

---

Now hold the picture, because familiarity has sanded the terror off it.

They had to walk into that. Between two walls of standing water, held up by
nothing they could see, on a seabed that had been under the sea an hour ago, with
an army entering the corridor behind them. Every step was taken underneath a
suspended weight that ordinary experience insisted was about to fall.

The way out was not the removal of the danger. The way out ran directly through the
middle of the thing that could kill them.

That is the shape of it more often than we admit. We keep hoping God will make the
sea go away, and instead He opens it, which requires us to walk in. The parting is
a mercy and it is also a demand. You cannot cross a parted sea from the shore.

---

Two temptations live at this point in the journey, and between them they account
for most failed disconnects.

**The first is the pull back to Egypt.**

Israel's complaints in the wilderness are worth reading closely, because they are
psychologically brilliant. We remember the fish which we ate in Egypt for nothing,
they say — for *nothing*, they say, of a country where their sons were being thrown
into the Nile. And they list the menu: cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, garlic.

That is nostalgia doing what nostalgia always does. It edits. It keeps the garlic
and deletes the whips. And it works, because memory under stress is not a recording
device, it is a comfort-seeking organ, and it will reliably serve you a version of
the past in which the thing you left was better than it was.

You will get that version. In the fourteenth month, when the new thing has not
arrived and the old thing has been thoroughly airbrushed by your own mind, you will
receive a very convincing case for going back. It will not feel like weakness. It
will feel like clarity — like you have finally stopped kidding yourself.

The defence is not willpower. It is written record. It is having told someone, at
the time, why you left, in specific and unedited terms, so that there is a witness
outside your own head when your head starts revising.

**The second is refusing to count the cost until it arrives.**

Jesus told the crowds to sit down and price the tower before laying the foundation.
The alternative, He said, is a half-built thing that everyone walking past can mock.
That is a strange thing for a man gathering followers to say, and it is the
kindest possible thing to say, because the person who is destroyed by a cost is
almost never destroyed by its size. They are destroyed by not having been told.

Count it now. What will this actually take from you — in money, in years, in
friendships, in reputation, in the good opinion of people whose good opinion you
have organised your life around? Write it down. It is far better to be daunted in
advance than blindsided at month eighteen.

---

There is a third thing, and it is not a temptation but a grief, and it does not get
enough room.

Naomi went to Moab because of a famine and came back with nothing. Her husband
dead, both sons dead, her position gone, walking into Bethlehem beside a foreign
daughter-in-law who had refused to leave her. And the women of the town come out
and say her name, and she stops them.

Do not call me Naomi, she says. Call me Mara — bitter — for the Almighty has dealt
very bitterly with me. I went out full, and the LORD has brought me home again
empty.

She is not being faithless. She is telling the truth in the presence of God about
what her life has cost her, and Scripture records it without a word of rebuke and
then spends the rest of the book quietly answering her. That is permission. If your
break has emptied you, you are allowed to say the word empty out loud, and you do
not have to append a hopeful clause to it in order to be received.

## In You

You may be standing at the water right now, and if you are, here is the only useful
thing anyone can tell you: the fear will not lift before you move.

That is the part people get wrong. They wait at the shore for the terror to
subside, on the theory that courage is a feeling that arrives and then produces
action. It is not. Israel walked between the walls of water afraid the entire way.
Nothing in the account suggests otherwise. The promise Moses gave them was not that
they would stop being frightened; it was that the ground would hold.

The ground will hold. The corridor is temporary — the water closed behind them
before the day was out — but the ground held while they were on it, and that is all
the ground ever has to do.

So the practical question in front of you is small and specific. Not *can I do
this*, which you cannot answer. What is the next step onto the seabed? One
conversation. One resignation letter. One appointment made. One honest sentence
said to one person. Disconnect is not accomplished by a leap; it is accomplished by
a walk, taken while afraid, in a place you did not think was walkable.

And keep the record. Write down, this week, in unglamorous detail, why you are
leaving. Date it. You will need it when the garlic starts to look good.

## In the Body

The Church has its own pull back to Egypt, and it is currently the strongest
undertow in Western Christianity.

It sounds like a decade. Sometimes the 1950s, when the buildings were full and the
culture agreed with us. Sometimes the fourth century, when an emperor made it easy.
Sometimes the 1970s or the 1990s or whichever era a particular movement peaked in.
The details vary; the structure never does. There was a time when this was working,
and the task now is to get back to it.

And the memory is doing exactly what Israel's did. The full buildings of the 1950s
in the West were full in part because attendance was socially compulsory, which is
not the same as a nation of disciples; and the same decade's church was capable of
sitting comfortably alongside segregation. The fourth-century settlement that ended
persecution also began the long confusion of the gospel with imperial power. Every
golden age has a Nile in it that nostalgia deletes.

The Body cannot go back, and the discovery that it cannot is one of the healthiest
things to happen to it in a century. Christendom is not available. The culture is
not returning. And every ounce of institutional energy spent on restoration is an
ounce not spent on the crossing.

What the Body needs at the water's edge is the same thing Israel needed: not the
removal of the danger, but the nerve to walk into it. Which in practice means
churches selling buildings they cannot fill and cannot heat, in order to be free to
be something else. Denominations letting structures die that are being sustained
by endowment rather than mission. Traditions accepting minority status in their own
homelands and discovering, as the Church has discovered in every other century and
on every other continent, that minority status is not the same as defeat.

The whole global Church already knows this. Only the West is still standing on the
shore.

## What Cannot Be Shaken

The corridor was temporary. That is the detail worth ending on.

The walls of water stood for a night and a morning and then came down; the road
they walked on does not exist and never did in any permanent sense. If Israel had
put their faith in the corridor they would have been putting their faith in a thing
with a lifespan of hours.

What held was not the road. It was the One who opened it and held it apart while
they crossed, and who was as present on the far shore as He had been on the near
one.

So do not put your weight on the arrangement. The particular provision that gets
you through this break — the job that came through, the friend who showed up, the
unexpected money, the door that opened at exactly the right week — is a corridor. It
will close. It was always going to close. What cannot be shaken is the hand that
parted the water, and that hand is still holding when the arrangement has long since
gone back to being sea.

## For Reflection

1. What is the actual next step onto the seabed? Not the whole crossing — the next
   step.
2. Write down, plainly, why you left. What has your memory already started editing?
3. Have you counted the cost, or have you avoided counting it because you suspect
   the number?
