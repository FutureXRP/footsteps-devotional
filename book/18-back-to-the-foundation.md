# Chapter Eighteen

## Back to the Foundation

Three hundred and eighteen bishops, by the traditional count, in a hall at Nicaea in
325, and some of them are carrying scars.

That detail matters more than the theology at first. These were men who had lived
through the last and worst persecution under Diocletian, and a number of them had
been mutilated for refusing to hand over the Scriptures — an eye put out, a hamstring
cut. And now an emperor has summoned them, paid for their travel, and is hosting
them, which is such a violent reversal of their circumstances that some of them must
have found the room hard to trust.

What they were there to settle was one question, and everything else in Christian
history depends on how it went.

A presbyter in Alexandria named Arius taught that the Son, however exalted, was a
creature — the first and greatest of God's works, but made, and therefore of a
different order from the Father. There was a time, the slogan ran, when He was not.
It was a reasonable position. It was philosophically tidier than the alternative. It
protected the oneness of God, which mattered enormously, and it was spreading fast.

Athanasius, then a young deacon, spent his life arguing that if it were true the
whole thing collapsed. If the Son is a creature, then a creature saved us, and
creatures cannot save. If the Son is a creature, then Christians worshipping Him are
idolaters. What was at stake was not a technicality about the divine nature; it was
whether the person Christians had been praying to for three hundred years was God or
not.

The council decided: of one being with the Father. Not similar — the same. And
Athanasius spent the following decades in and out of exile five times as the empire
changed its mind repeatedly, which is where the phrase *Athanasius against the
world* comes from.

A century later at Chalcedon they had to do the same work on the other side: one
person, two natures, truly God and truly man, without confusion, without change,
without division, without separation. Four negatives, fencing a mystery rather than
explaining it — telling you where the road has no shoulder, not what the country
looks like.

---

I put this chapter here, in the middle of Reorientation, deliberately.

Because there is a particular question that arrives when a person or a Church starts
rebuilding, and it is: **what am I actually building on?**

In the wilderness, everything is up for grabs and nothing is settled, and that is
appropriate. But you cannot build on everything being up for grabs. At some point
the reconstruction requires you to decide what is foundation and what is
superstructure — what you would die for and what you now hold loosely, and which of
the things you inherited belong in which category.

That sorting is the most important intellectual work of this phase, and almost
nobody is taught how to do it.

The creeds are a gift precisely here. They are short. Notice how short. The Nicene
Creed takes ninety seconds to say, and it does not mention the age of the earth,
church government, the mode of baptism, the order of the end times, spiritual gifts,
or any of the questions that Christians have spent the last two centuries dividing
over. It is the Church's own answer to the question of what is load-bearing, worked
out under pressure, by people who had been tortured for the faith and were not
inclined to be casual about it.

If you have come out of a system that treated fifty things as essential, the creed
is a shock. It is so much smaller than what you were handed. That is not a lowering
of the bar. That is the actual bar, established by the Church at the moments it was
being forced to say what it could not live without.

---

The Reformation's sorting was the same exercise conducted again a millennium later,
and its results are usually reduced to a list.

Grace alone. Faith alone. Scripture alone. Christ alone. Glory to God alone.

Reduced to a list they are slogans. Read as answers to specific questions about what
is foundational, they are something else.

**Grace alone** answers: what is the ground of my standing with God? Not my
contribution, not partly mine and partly His. A gift, entire.

**Faith alone** answers: how is it received? Not by achievement but by empty hands
— resting on someone else's righteousness rather than assembling your own. Luther's
term for it was an alien righteousness, meaning one from outside you, which is
exactly what a person who has failed in the wilderness needs to hear.

**Scripture alone** answers the question of authority — the question this book keeps
returning to. Not that tradition and reason and experience count for nothing; the
reformers used all three constantly. But that there is one voice which stands over
the others and can correct them, and to which appeal can be made when the
institution has gone wrong.

**Christ alone** answers: who mediates? One mediator between God and men, the man
Christ Jesus. No queue, no intermediary, no one whose approval is required before
you can approach.

**Glory to God alone** answers what the whole thing is for, and it is the one that
reorients everything else, because it removes you from the centre of your own
restoration project.

And the honest historical footnote: the Catholic reform that followed at Trent was
not merely a reaction. It reformed genuine abuses, ended the traffic in indulgences
that started the whole thing, required bishops to actually live in their dioceses,
and produced a spiritual renewal — Teresa, John of the Cross, Ignatius — of a depth
that any tradition would envy. Both sides did foundation work in that century. Only
one side of that is usually taught, depending which pew you grew up in.

## In You

Do the sorting. Explicitly, on paper.

Three columns. **What I would die for.** **What I hold with conviction and could be
wrong about.** **What I have discovered I do not actually care about.**

Almost everyone who does this honestly finds that the first column is much shorter
than they expected, and that a startling number of things they were prepared to
fight about a decade ago have quietly relocated to the third.

That is not a loss of conviction. It is the recovery of proportion, which is what
this phase is for. A faith in which everything is equally essential is not a strong
faith; it is an undifferentiated one, and it shatters as a single unit when any
piece of it fails. That is precisely why so many deconstructions are total — a
person raised to believe that a young earth and the resurrection stood or fell
together, discovering a problem with the first, correctly concluded that the second
was in trouble too, because that is what they were taught.

Sort it, and a great deal of what has been shaking turns out to have been shaking in
the third column.

Then build the first column properly. Learn the creed — actually learn it, so it is
available at three in the morning. Read one of the great sorters. Augustine, who
spent decades on the two cities and the restless heart. Julian of Norwich, whose
insistence that all shall be well was written by a woman in an anchorhold during the
Black Death, not by an optimist. Thomas à Kempis. They are all long dead, which is
their advantage: they are not participants in your current arguments, and they were
sorting under pressures that make ours look small.

## In the Body

The Church's foundation-sorting is its most valuable inheritance and its most
neglected one.

The great councils and confessions were written in exactly the situation Western
Christianity is in now: under pressure, in the middle of a crisis of authority, when
it had become necessary to say what could not be given up. They are the Body's
accumulated answer to the question this rummage sale is asking.

And a large part of the Western Church has no working relationship with them. A
generation of evangelicals was raised with a high view of Scripture and no
functioning connection to the first five centuries of the people who assembled it,
which produced a faith that was simultaneously very confident and very shallow —
sure about a great many secondary things and unable to say why the Trinity mattered.
When that faith met serious pressure, it had no floor.

The recovery is happening, and it is one of the genuine signs of reorientation in
the Body: young Christians reading the fathers, congregations reciting creeds their
parents thought were dead formalism, a serious retrieval of ancient practice across
traditions that had discarded it. Not as nostalgia — as ballast.

But the same sorting exposes something the Body would rather not look at. A great
deal of what currently divides Western Christianity is superstructure being defended
as foundation. Positions on political questions have acquired, in some quarters, a
functional status higher than the creed — you can deny the resurrection in some
congregations more safely than you can vote wrongly. That is a foundation error, and
it is doing more damage to the Church's witness than any external pressure.

The council at Nicaea had the humility to write ninety seconds and stop. That
restraint is the model.

## What Cannot Be Shaken

Paul told a fracturing congregation that no one can lay any other foundation than
the one already laid, which is Jesus Christ — and then, in the next breath, that
what people build on it gets tested by fire, with the wood and hay and stubble
burning off.

The councils were not laying the foundation. They could not; it was laid. They were
inspecting what had been built and marking where the load actually ran, and they did
it under conditions where getting it wrong would have cost everything.

What cannot be shaken is that the foundation was laid without you and is not on
trial. Your sorting is not a test of whether the ground will hold. It is the far
less frightening work of finding out which of the things you were standing on is
actually the ground.

## For Reflection

1. Do the three columns. What is in the first, honestly? Is it shorter than you
   expected?
2. What have you been treating as foundation that turns out to be superstructure?
3. Could you say the creed from memory? What would it mean to have it available at
   three in the morning?
