### A map for living through the shaking

## A note before you begin

This book was written for two kinds of reader, and it refuses to choose between
them.

The first is a person whose own ground has moved. Something broke — a marriage, a
body, a career, a certainty, a faith you were handed as a child and can no longer
hold in the shape it came in. You are not looking for a system. You are looking
for a sentence that tells you where you are, and whether anyone has been here
before, and whether there is anything on the other side.

The second is anyone paying attention to what is happening to the Christian faith
in the West. Membership numbers falling for a generation. Institutions that felt
permanent in 1975 looking brittle now. Authority contested everywhere — which
Bible, whose reading, which tradition, which voice. Meanwhile the faith grows
faster in Lagos and Seoul and São Paulo than it has grown anywhere in centuries,
and the centre of gravity of world Christianity slides south and east while
Western Christians argue about the furniture.

These are not two subjects. They are one subject, and the whole argument of this
book is that they share a shape. What breaks a soul and what breaks a church move
through the same four phases, ask the same questions in the same order, and are
tempted by the same shortcuts. Learn the map in one, and you can read the other.

So every chapter turns twice. It turns toward you, personally, under the heading
**In You** — your own shaking and nothing else. And it turns toward the Church as
a whole, under **In the Body** — not one congregation somewhere, but the body of
Christ across the world and across the centuries, currently in the middle of
something enormous. The two are kept clean and separate on purpose. Blur them and
you get sermons that use your grief to make a point about institutions, or
institutional analysis that never lands on a human being. Keep them apart and each
one can say what it actually has to say.

## How to read this

Straight through, if you can. The book is built as one journey, and the parts do
not work well out of order — Reorientation reads like empty optimism if you have
not sat in Disorientation, which is exactly the mistake the phase itself invites.

But if you have picked this up because you are in the middle of something, go to
the part that names where you are. There is no virtue in reading about
reconnection while you are still bleeding from the break.

Each chapter ends with three questions. They are slow questions. They are not
comprehension checks, and the book does not need you to answer them to keep going.
They are there because upheaval is not survived by understanding it.

## One caution, stated once and meant throughout

The governing promise of this book is that God shakes what He means to keep. That
is Hebrews, and it is true, and it will be repeated.

It is not the same as saying God authored what happened to you.

Where this book touches inflicted evil — betrayal, abuse, atrocity, the death of a
child — it will hold two things at once, and it will not let go of either. God is
not the author of evil. When someone sinned against you, the guilt is theirs and
it does not get quietly transferred to heaven's account. And yet nothing that
reaches you falls outside His hand, and He is able to take what was meant for harm
and weave it toward life. He did not send it. He is Lord of the wreckage anyway.

If you are carrying something that makes the word *shaking* feel like an
understatement, read that paragraph again before you go on. The rest of the book
assumes it.
