# PART THREE — REORIENTATION

> *New bearings. Something truer takes shape, slowly, from underneath.*

Not the old thing restored. Not relief. Reconstruction — which is slower than
collapse, less dramatic, and requires decisions about what to build with. Six
chapters: how the turn begins, what identity is recovered, how rebuilding actually
proceeds, what the foundations are, how the scale of values gets redrawn, and what
happens when a whole Church finds its bearings again.

---

# Chapter Sixteen

## A Voice Behind You

The turn, when it comes, almost never announces itself.

Isaiah is speaking to a people who have exhausted every strategy. They have run to
Egypt for help, made alliances, trusted horses and chariots, done everything a small
nation does when it is frightened, and none of it has worked. The chapter is a long
indictment of a people who wanted their prophets to tell them smooth things.

And then, in the middle of it, this:

Your ears will hear a word behind you, saying, This is the way. Walk in it, when
you turn to the right hand, and when you turn to the left.

Behind you. Not ahead, where you were looking. Not above, in the thunder you were
listening for. Behind — the way a guide walks behind a person on an unfamiliar
path, close enough to speak at conversational volume, correcting the drift rather
than announcing the route.

This is the way. Walk in it.

That is what reorientation sounds like at the beginning, and it is why so many
people miss it. You spend a wilderness listening for something enormous, on the
reasonable assumption that a God who could end this would do it loudly. And what
eventually comes is a quiet instruction about the next hundred yards, from a
direction you were not facing.

---

Elijah gets the same lesson in a more explicit form, and it is the passage that
should be read alongside this one.

After the broom tree, after the bread and the sleep, he walks forty days to Horeb —
Sinai, the mountain — and gets into a cave. God asks what he is doing there and he
delivers his complaint: he alone is left, and they are seeking his life. He is told
to go out and stand on the mountain, because the LORD is passing by.

And there is a wind so strong it tears the mountain apart. The LORD was not in the
wind. Then an earthquake. The LORD was not in the earthquake. Then a fire — and
this must have been almost unbearable for Elijah, who had watched fire fall on
Carmel and knew exactly what God in a fire looked like. The LORD was not in the
fire.

And after the fire, a still small voice. A sound of gentle stillness, one older
translation has it; a thin whisper, in the Hebrew's most literal sense.

He had just been shown the three things he was equipped to recognise, and God was
in none of them. The man whose entire ministry had been built on spectacular
demonstration was reoriented by being taught to listen for something quiet.

---

Here is the practical shape of how the turn actually arrives, drawn from every
account I know of, biblical and otherwise.

**It is retrospective before it is prospective.** Almost nobody notices
reorientation beginning. They notice, three or four months later, that something
has changed — that a passage of Scripture landed for the first time in two years,
that they got through a Tuesday without the weight, that they wanted to pray rather
than made themselves. The turn is usually detected in the rear-view mirror.

**It is small.** Zechariah's word to a demoralised remnant trying to rebuild a
temple that would be pitifully inferior to Solomon's is a rebuke to everyone waiting
for something impressive: who has despised the day of small things? Then the
image — they will rejoice to see the plumb line in the hand of Zerubbabel. Not the
finished building. A measuring tool in a man's hand, which is to say the very first
evidence that anyone is building at all.

**It comes with correction, not just comfort.** The voice behind you says *this is
the way*, and it says it specifically when you turn to the right hand or the left.
It is a drift-correction mechanism. If everything you are hearing from God is
consolation and nothing is adjustment, be a little suspicious.

**And it does not undo the wilderness.** This is the part people find hardest. The
new orientation is not the old one restored with the pain removed. Israel came out
of the wilderness with a law, a tabernacle and an identity they did not have going
in, and also with an entire generation buried behind them. The turn is real. It is
not a reversal.

---

Isaiah has one more image for this and it is the one to carry out of the chapter.

Behold, I will do a new thing. Now it springs forth. Don't you know it? I will even
make a way in the wilderness, and rivers in the desert.

*Now it springs forth.* Present tense, spoken to people who could not see it. And
then the question, which is almost impatient: don't you know it? Don't you perceive
it?

The image is of something germinating. Not arriving — germinating, which is a
process that happens entirely underground and is invisible for a period before any
green shows. By the time you can see a shoot, the actual work has been going on for
weeks in the dark.

Which means the honest answer to *don't you perceive it* is very often no. No, I do
not. And the passage does not treat that as a failure. It treats it as the normal
condition of someone standing over ground where something has already started.

## In You

Look backwards. That is the first instruction, and it is the one that will show you
something.

Take the last six months and ask what is different — not what has improved, which is
a different and more demanding question, but what is *different*. Is there a
question that used to torment you and now merely interests you? A person you can
think about without the old spike? A passage that opened? A capacity for ordinary
enjoyment that came back without being summoned? Most people who do this exercise
honestly find at least one thing, and most of them had not noticed it.

Then, lower your expectations of what the turn will feel like. If you are waiting
for the wind, the earthquake or the fire, you may be standing in the middle of a
reorientation and filing it as nothing, because it does not match the scale of what
you went through. The break was loud. The rebuild is quiet. That asymmetry is not a
sign that God is being stingy with you; it is simply how construction differs from
demolition.

And be careful of one specific thing here, because this is where a good phase goes
wrong. The relief of the first new bearings is enormous, and there is a strong
temptation to seize the first coherent framework that comes along and hold it as
tightly as you held the old one — to leave the wilderness with a new certainty
rather than with a truer humility. That is how people go from one rigid system to
its mirror image and call it growth. The voice behind you gives you the next
stretch of path. It does not hand you a map of the whole country, and if someone is
offering you one, they are offering you something God did not.

## In the Body

The Body's reorientations begin the same way and are almost always
under-recognised at the time.

Nobody in the sixth century watched a handful of men at Monte Cassino agree on when
to sleep and who would do the washing up and thought they were looking at the
mechanism by which Western civilisation would keep its books. Nobody watching an Augustinian friar lecture on Romans in a
provincial university in 1515 thought they were watching the next five centuries
begin. The plumb line in Zerubbabel's hand is what renewal looks like from inside:
a small tool, in an ordinary hand, on a site that nobody impressive is visiting.

So the question for the Church now is not where the great movement is. It is where
the small tools are. And there are candidates, if you are willing to look at
unfashionable places: the quiet recovery of the daily office and fixed-hour prayer
by young Christians whose parents had no liturgy; the sheer scale of ordinary
congregational care that never appears in any statistic; the renewal running
through the global South that Western institutions barely track; the church-planting
happening in immigrant communities in European cities that the historic
denominations have not noticed is happening in their own postcodes.

And the same caution scales up. The Church's temptation in early reorientation is
to seize a confident framework quickly, because the wilderness has been exhausting
and certainty sells. Every generation of Christian upheaval produces movements that
mistake volume for bearings. The test Isaiah offers is whether the new thing
corrects you as well as comforts you — whether it says *this is the way* when you
drift, or only ever tells you that you were right.

## What Cannot Be Shaken

There is someone behind you.

Strip the chapter to its floor and that is what is left, and it is enough. Not a
map, not an explanation, not a schedule. A voice at close range, on a path you do
not know, from a direction you were not watching, that has been there the whole
time you thought you were navigating alone.

What cannot be shaken is not your ability to perceive the new thing. You may not
perceive it for another year. It is that the new thing is already springing forth,
underground, in ground you have written off — and the One who planted it is walking
close enough behind you to speak.

## For Reflection

1. What is different about you now compared with six months ago? Not better —
   different.
2. Have you been listening for the wind, the earthquake and the fire? What have you
   filed as nothing?
3. Is what you are hearing from God correcting you, or only comforting you?
