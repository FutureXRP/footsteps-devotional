# Production plan

**Drafted:** ~11,500 words. **Target:** 55–65,000.

Draft to the end before any polishing. Three modifications to that, below.

## Order of writing

| # | Section | Est. | Notes |

| — | *The Waste* | 7,600 ✅ | Arc complete. Thin; will thicken in revision, not now. |
| 1 | **Slow Country** (rest) | ~10,000 | Constant's arc, and the country's own work on him. |

### Why the Sundering goes last

It is the opening country — the break, the four months of stone — and it is the
one part of the book that does not need discovering. Written now it would be
guesswork about a man we do not yet fully know; written last it can be shaped to
the man who came out the other end, and cut to the bone, because *you still say it
in months* already contains those four months and the reader will not need them
dramatised. Lean is the goal. The garden weights it retroactively.

### Why the Return is the next risk point

It is the scene the entire book is engineered toward, and it carries four things
that must all land at once: she does not need rescuing; the wall keeps his worst
work; the traveller at her table is not his replacement; and the sentence stopped
on the step gets spent or refused. If that scene does not work, the Table Lands is
being written toward nothing.

Same logic that put the Waste first. Draft it before the Table Lands, accept it
will be re-drafted once the middle exists, and treat it as a second de-risking
move rather than a finished chapter.

---

## Review cadence — cut it back

Every installment was right while the concept was unproven. It is now expensive
and slightly dangerous: reviewing 2,500 words at a time invites polish, and polish
before the shape exists is wasted work on scenes that may not survive.

**New cadence: one review per country.** End of the Slow Country. After the
Return. End of the Table Lands. Then a full-manuscript read before revision.

Four rounds instead of eighteen.

---

## The continuity ledger — checked every installment, not at the end

The compass error proved these do not survive on memory. Running list:

- **The map** — entry from the east, the shared nine-day leg, divergence past the
  fourth rise, and *the burnt scrub on the right* saved for the return.
- **Eleven** — reserved for what descends from Grieve. Everything else recounted.
- **Six feet** — spent. Four uses, the last one crossed. Do not use it again.
- **The unspent sentence** — stopped on the step in Eighteen. Still in his mouth.
  It is the return's loaded object. Do not spend it early.
- **Ash** — never resolved. No traveller with news, no flask, nothing, ever.
- **The bulge** — the wall keeps his worst work and the book never says why.
- **The Caravan** — seen once, uninterpreted. Never returned to.

## What revision will actually be

Deferred, not avoided. When the draft is done:

1. **Thicken the Waste** if the finished book makes it feel thin.
2. **Line pass for the two habits** — prophetic retrospection, and anyone
   accidentally speaking in portable wisdom.
3. **The title decision.** Before the edit, not after — it affects the last page.
4. **Human readers.** This is the one thing no amount of drafting substitutes for.
   Specifically: someone with trauma history on the Waste and the dark-night
   material, and at least one reader who left the faith and did not come back.
