# Full audit before outside review

Read end to end, 38,197 words, 78 sections, 117 pages.

## Verdict

**Ready to be read by other people. Not ready to publish.**

Those are different thresholds and the gap between them is real. The book is
finished in the sense that every scene it needs exists, the voice holds from the
first page to the last, and nothing in it is placeholder. It is not finished in
the sense that no human being has read it and it has never had a line edit.
Sending it out now is exactly the right move. Putting it on Amazon now would not
be.

What I actually think of it, since that was the question: the second half of Part
Two is the best writing in either book, fiction or non-fiction. Grieve works.
*You still say it in months*, the second barrel, *that is not knowing, that is
being told*, *nobody has asked me* — she is doing the thing the whole design
depended on, which is being devastating while talking about water. Constant is
the warmest thing in it and the cache argument is the first place the book lets
two decent people be irreconcilable without resolving it. The twelve-mile road
that goes from a well to a field is the best single idea in the project. And the
ending refuses every consolation it is offered, which is what the non-fiction
spent 53,000 words earning the right to do.

## Defects found and fixed in this pass

Fourteen real ones. The first three would have been caught by any careful
reader and would have cost the book credibility.

1. **Grieve used a name she had never heard.** She said *I am not Ivo*, and then
   Steadfast said *you do not know who Ivo is*, and she agreed. The beat had
   collapsed at some point in drafting. Rewritten so he says the name without
   thinking and she catches the *manner* rather than the word — which is better
   than what was there.
2. **Rell's slate was a gun on the mantel that never fired.** She puts a blank
   slate in his hand with an explicit instruction — get somebody to write down
   what Grieve knows before she dies — and he says *I will*, and it is never
   mentioned again. Now paid, at ~450 words, and paid awkwardly rather than
   neatly: Grieve is impossible to dictate, everything she knows comes out as *it
   depends*, and the only thing that goes down cleanly is the route, which takes
   her a minute, because it is the one thing she has ever had to give anybody.
   She turns out to have been able to write the whole time. *Who is it for* is
   the question that had stopped her, and his answer takes him a week and is not
   clever.
3. **Steadfast's age did not add up.** Named at forty-one, *Steadfast for seven of
   the nineteen years*, which put him at sixty-one on the last page, where the
   text says fifty-nine. Changed to six. Grieve's twenty-one years is now
   twenty-two, which is what eleven-plus-two-plus-nine actually comes to.
4. **Grieve's count was stale in Part Four.** Steadfast tells the valley *she has
   nine*, but she had thirteen by the time he left. Now thirteen. He stays the
   ninth.
5. **Sal aged unevenly** — eight in the first winter, then ten, then thirteen,
   then fourteen, on a timeline that only allows one of those. Now seven / eight
   on the kerb / ten / twelve / thirteen, which is consistent and keeps
   *eight-year-old on a kerb* true in Part Five.
6. **Elapsed time.** *Two and a half years* appeared three times as a fixed tag,
   including once in the valley's third year, where the true figure was six. All
   three re-set.
7. **Corr was fat in the middle of a country where everybody arrives half dead.**
   The text pointed at it and never answered it. He is now three weeks on the
   fringe rather than four months in it, which also explains why he leaves south.
8. **Bel.** *He had eaten at the same table as her ninety times* — the valley eats
   together every evening; the real number is over a thousand.
9. **Grieve's second laugh** was phrased so it read as a third.
10. **The nine.** The book's title number appeared sixty-one times in 37,500
    words, most of them as a generic small number: nine people at a staging house,
    nine things in a workshop, nine versions of a conversation, nine days of
    store, kerb nine short. Down to forty-six. Every survivor is load-bearing —
    the nine days of the route, the nine years of the road, the nine years since
    the garden, and the ninth.
11. **Typography.** Every section heading in the PDF was preceded by a stray
    scene-break ornament. Fixed in the build.
12. The hut arithmetic (fifteen months / nineteen / twelve days ago), the
    *there had never been a method* over-explanation, and the too-clean
    both-things conversation, all from the previous pass.

## What is genuinely not ready

- ~~No title decision.~~ **Settled: *The Country Between*.** See DECISIONS.md.
  The proof and the build now carry it.
- **No line edit.** *which was* runs to about fifty instances and is the
  narrator's characteristic hinge — load-bearing, and also a tic. *That was the
  thing / that was all / that was the whole of it* is the same story. This wants
  a pen and a printed page, not a regex, and it should happen after the reviews,
  not before.
- **Length.** 38,000 is a short novel. Sellable, but slight. If reviewers want
  more, the honest places to find it are the four months in the Slow Country and
  the seven years in the valley — not anywhere in Part Two.
- **Nobody has read it.** That is the whole point of this stage and no amount of
  self-auditing substitutes.

## Questions worth putting to the reviewers

Ask these explicitly, or the notes will come back on things that are already
decided.

1. **Part Three.** The engine of the book — *will he go back* — dies at the end
   of Part Two and does not restart until the sixth year of Part Four. Part Three
   is carried entirely by Constant. Does it hold, or does the book sag between the
   garden and the road?
2. **Part Four.** Seven years in 6,400 words, no protagonist crisis, and a
   community that works. Is it the heart of the book or the place where it becomes
   an essay about community with dialogue in it?
3. **The narrator.** Part One is told by somebody looking back — *he will not be
   interesting about the journey*, *he would like it said plainly*. That frame is
   loud at the start, nearly silent through the middle, and returns in Part Five.
   Deliberate, or does it need to be either consistent or gone?
4. **The refusals.** Ash is never resolved. Til is never explained. The bulge is
   never accounted for. The Caravan is seen once and not understood. Nobody
   connects the three fires. Does the accumulation read as discipline or as a
   mannerism by the third time?
5. **Two women walk off and are never heard of again** — Maud and Ash. That is a
   deliberate pair (one where Grieve withheld and was right, one where she
   answered and was wrong) but it is still twice. Does it land as design?
6. **The last page.** *None of them knew about each other yet.* Is that an ending
   or a withholding?

## Deliberate — please do not let reviewers talk you out of these

- No quotation marks anywhere; dialogue is em-dashed.
- Nobody is ever aware of saying anything wise.
- No prophetic retrospection — the narrator may look back at the last hour and
  never forward from it.
- The road is never explained and never comes back.
- Grieve is not rescued, does not leave, and has not been waiting.
- The three fires are never linked, and the failure to link them is the ending.
